# Purple cow cold outreach templates

Seth Godin's purple cow idea: a field of brown cows is scenery, nobody points a phone at it. The one purple cow gets remembered and talked about. Most cold outreach is a brown cow, safe and professional-sounding, which is exactly why it gets ignored. Every template below leads with something specific enough that it could only have gone to this one prospect, since that's the only kind of opener that earns a reply.

Two things before the templates:

- You asked for four and described three (high-converting, short DM, premium/consultant). I added a follow-up as the fourth, since it's the natural next step once the first three go unanswered.
- Fields use double-curly merge tags ({{like_this}}) so they drop straight into an n8n workflow or a mail-merge tool. Replace them by hand if you're sending one at a time.

## Fields you'll need

- {{your_name}}, {{your_brand}}, {{type_of_business}} (who you work with, e.g. "boutique retailers"), {{proof_point}} (one real number or reaction from past work), {{portfolio_link}}
- {{prospect_first_name}}, {{prospect_business}}, {{industry}}, {{competitor_a}}, {{competitor_b}}
- {{specific_observation}}, {{second_observation}}, {{specific_detail}}: the fields the whole approach depends on. These need to be true and checkable about this exact prospect. A generic compliment is the brown cow here. A specific, checkable detail is the purple one.

## Template 1: high-converting email

Subject: {{competitor_a}} and {{competitor_b}} look exactly like you

Hi {{prospect_first_name}},

I looked at three {{industry}} websites this morning: yours, {{competitor_a}}'s, and {{competitor_b}}'s. Same color palette, same stock photo of people shaking hands, and a headline that says "trusted" or "reliable," like every other site in the category. Cover up the logos and I couldn't tell you which one was which.

That's not really a design problem so much as a memory problem: nothing here gives a visitor a reason to remember you five minutes after closing the tab, and that's quietly costing {{prospect_business}} the customers who are already comparing you against everyone else on their shortlist.

{{prospect_business}} doesn't need a rebrand that makes it look more professional. Professional is the bar everyone already clears. What gets you chosen is being the one people point at and ask who did that.

I'm {{your_name}}. I work with {{type_of_business}} on brand identity that gets talked about instead of just approved in a meeting. {{proof_point}}.

Would fifteen minutes be worth your time? I'll bring three specific things about {{prospect_business}}'s current brand that are making it blend in right now. Free, whether we end up working together or not.

{{your_name}}
{{portfolio_link}}

## Template 2: short DM (354 characters as written)

Hey {{prospect_first_name}}, went through {{prospect_business}}'s page. It's clean, but so is every other {{industry}} page right now, same fonts, same layout, different name at the top. I help brands stop blending in. Got two minutes? I've got one specific thing about your current brand worth flagging. No pitch, just the observation, yours either way.

## Template 3: premium positioning (branding consultant, not "just a designer")

Subject: A positioning question, not a design pitch

Hi {{prospect_first_name}},

Most people who reach out to {{prospect_business}} want to redesign your logo. I want to ask a different question: why should someone pick {{prospect_business}} over the four or five other options they're already comparing you to?

That's a positioning question, not a design question, and it's the difference between a brand that competes on price and one that's the only real option in the room.

I work as a brand strategist, and design is the last step in what I do, not the first. Before anyone opens a design file, we figure out what makes {{prospect_business}} the version of {{industry}} that people remember and mention to friends without being asked, the purple cow in a field of brown ones, if you'll forgive the reference.

If that's worth fifteen minutes of your time, I'd like it on your calendar. I'll come with a short, specific read on where {{prospect_business}}'s current positioning is strong and where it's quietly costing you.

{{your_name}}
Brand Strategy & Identity
{{portfolio_link}}

## Template 4: follow-up

Subject: One more thing I noticed

Hi {{prospect_first_name}},

Didn't hear back, which is fine. {{prospect_business}}'s inbox is probably not short on people trying to sell you something.

One more thing though: I looked at {{specific_detail}} again this morning and noticed {{second_observation}}. Thought it was worth mentioning even if we never end up working together.

If you want the fifteen-minute version of what I'd change, the offer still stands. If not, I'll leave it here and won't follow up again.

{{your_name}}

## Using these well

The templates fail if {{specific_observation}} turns into generic flattery like "I love your brand." They work if it's something the prospect can check in five seconds and think "how did they notice that." That single detail is doing almost all the work; everything else here is just structure.
